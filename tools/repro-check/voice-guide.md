## Who I am in threads

<!-- 2-3 lines. Who is talking when you comment on an issue: your

I am a full-stack software engineer with 4+ years of experience building web applications and microservices with React and Node.js. I am starting my first contribution to this open-source project and maintainers can expect me to be helpful, knowledgeable, and professional.

## Rules I write by

<!-- 3-5 rules, drafted from the lecture's slide-12 moment. Each rule

### Rule: No promised timelines

- Wrong: "I'll get the repro report up later today"
- Right: "I'll get the repro report up once I confirm the behavior on my end."

### Rule: Name the version and behavior specifically

- Wrong: "I'm getting an error message."
- Right: "Error code 404 is thrown opening the app on version 1.2.3."

### Rule: Use professional language without emotional color

- Wrong: "Omg I love this project! Can't wait to contribute!"
- Right: "I will be picking up this issue as my first contribution to this project."

## Rule: Do not ask questions about the project already answered

- Wrong: "What is the proper way to contribute to this project?"
- Right: "I will follow the contribution guidelines."

## Rule: Speak with certainty

- Wrong: "I don't think I can reproduce the issue properly."
- Right: "I could not reproduce the issue on my OS and tool versions."

## Things I never post

<!-- A short list. Promises you cannot keep, tones you refuse,
shortcuts you know you reach for when tired. The skill quotes this
list back at you when a draft crosses it. -->

- Do not give specific dates or times.
- Do not write excessively enthusiastic or excited (many exclamation marks).
- Do not use em dashes.
- Do not use first person plural as a contributor to a project.
- Do not open with a compliment about the project.
